A translated page can be correct and still look wrong. Usually it is the type.

## Line length

Sixty-eight characters is a comfortable measure in English. It is comfortable in
French and Spanish too, which is lucky, and slightly tight in German, which is
not. We set the measure in characters rather than pixels so it adapts on its own
instead of pretending every language has the same average word.

## Quotation marks

English uses curly doubles. French uses guillemets, with a thin space inside them.
German uses low-then-high. A translation that keeps the English marks reads as a
translation on sight, before anyone has processed a word of it.

This is the sort of detail worth putting in writing for whoever, or whatever, does
the translating. It is invisible to a spell check and obvious to a reader.

## Numerals and dates

Format dates from a single source rather than translating them as text. A date
written out by a translator is a date that will eventually disagree with the one
in the file, and the one in the file is the one the sitemap uses.

## One typeface is enough

We use a single family at three weights across the whole site. Every hour we have
spent choosing a second one has been an hour we did not spend on the measure, the
rhythm, or the spacing around a heading, which are what readers actually notice.
